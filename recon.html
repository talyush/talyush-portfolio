Reconnaissance

The art of seeing what others miss

Reconnaissance (recon) is the systematic process of gathering information about a target before any exploitation attempt. It's about seeing - mapping out the digital landscape, understanding the terrain, and identifying potential entry points.

Why Recon Matters

In security, what you can't see can hurt you. Proper reconnaissance reveals:

  • Hidden services and exposed ports
  • Misconfigured security settings
  • Forgotten backup files and directories
  • Outdated software versions
  • Employee information and organizational structure

The Recon Process

1
Passive Recon
Gathering information without direct interaction - OSINT, search engines, public records
2
Active Recon
Direct interaction with the target - port scanning, service enumeration, banner grabbing
3
Vulnerability Assessment
Identifying potential weaknesses based on gathered information
4
Documentation
Mapping findings and creating attack vectors

Tools of the Trade

Nmap: Network mapping and port scanning

Shodan: Internet-connected device search engine

theHarvester: Email, subdomain and people harvesting

Maltego: Visual link analysis tool

Recon-ng: Full-featured Web Reconnaissance framework

The Philosophy of Seeing

"Reconnaissance is not about finding vulnerabilities - it's about understanding the target deeply enough to know where vulnerabilities might exist."

The best security researchers see patterns where others see chaos. They understand that every piece of information, no matter how small, contributes to the bigger picture.

Ethical Considerations

Reconnaissance should always be conducted with proper authorization. Unauthorized reconnaissance can be illegal and may be interpreted as a precursor to an attack.

  • Only scan systems you have permission to test
  • Document your findings responsibly
  • Report vulnerabilities through proper channels
  • Respect privacy and confidentiality
