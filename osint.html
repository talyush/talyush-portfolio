OSINT

Open Source Intelligence - Seeing through public data

OSINT (Open Source Intelligence) is the practice of gathering information from publicly available sources to understand, analyze, and make decisions about targets. It's about seeing patterns and connections that others miss in the vast ocean of public data.

The Philosophy of OSINT

"Everything leaves a trace. The question is whether you know how to follow it."

Key Information Sources

Social Media
Profiles, connections, posts, photos, location data
Public Records
Business registrations, property records, court documents
Technical Data
DNS records, WHOIS data, IP geolocation
Search Engines
Advanced queries, cached pages, indexed content

Common OSINT Techniques

  • Username enumeration: Finding all accounts associated with a username across platforms
  • Email investigation: Tracing email addresses to linked accounts and breaches
  • Domain research: Analyzing domain registration and hosting information
  • Image analysis: Reverse image search and metadata extraction
  • Geolocation: Determining physical locations from digital footprints

Tools for OSINT

Maltego: Visual link analysis and relationship mapping

Shodan: Internet-connected device search engine

theHarvester: Email, subdomain and people harvesting

SpiderFoot: Automated OSINT framework

Google Dorks: Advanced search operators for targeted reconnaissance

Ethical Considerations

OSINT can be powerful but must be used responsibly:

  • Only gather information you have a legitimate need for
  • Respect privacy laws and regulations
  • Don't use OSINT for harassment or stalking
  • Document your sources and methodology
  • Report findings through appropriate channels

The Art of Seeing

"OSINT isn't about finding secrets - it's about seeing what's already visible but overlooked. Every piece of public data tells a story if you know how to read it."

The most effective OSINT practitioners think like investigators - curious, methodical, and always looking for connections that others miss.
